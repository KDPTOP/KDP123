## How the price band works

Your list price must fall inside the band for the royalty option you pick. On the 35% option, the US floor depends on file size: $0.99 under 3 MB, $1.99 from 3 MB to under 10 MB, and $2.99 at 10 MB or more, with a $200 ceiling. On the 70% option the US band is $2.99 to $12.99 regardless of file size. The $12.99 ceiling took effect on July 7, 2026, replacing the $9.99 cap Amazon says had been in place since 2007. Nobody was required to reprice.

## The print edition rule

If you choose 70%, the Digital Book Pricing Page requires your ebook list price to be at least 20% below the Amazon list price of any physical edition. A paperback listed at $14.99 therefore caps the 70% ebook at $11.99, even though the band itself allows $12.99.

## Price matching

Amazon sets the retail price and may sell your ebook below your list price to match a third party's price for any digital or physical edition, or Amazon's own price for a print edition. On the 70% option, royalties are then calculated on the lower sale price. If you make the book free elsewhere, Amazon may match that too, and the royalty during that period is zero.

## Worked example

A 4 MB ebook on the 35% option needs a US list price of at least $1.99. Moving it to 70% requires at least $2.99 and no more than $12.99, or 20% below the paperback, whichever is lower.

## Common mistakes

- Expecting the detail page to show your list price: Amazon sets the retail price. On the 35% option royalties stay based on your list price; on the 70% option they follow the actual sale price.
- Setting different EU prices per store: customers who can buy from several EU stores get the lowest of them.
- Forgetting fixed price laws: in Austria, Belgium, France, Germany and Greece the ebook price must match across retailers.

Amazon changes these rules; the linked KDP help pages are the authority.
