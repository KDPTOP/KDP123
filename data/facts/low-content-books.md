## What KDP treats as low-content

A low-content book has minimal or no interior content and is generally repetitive, designed to be filled in by the user. Amazon's examples include notebooks, planners, diaries and journals, prompt journals, log books, coupon books, score card templates, crafting templates and blank sheet music. Puzzle books, coloring books, photography, sheet music, manuals, textbooks and children's books are generally not low-content, though Amazon notes there can be exceptions.

## Setting one up

Low-content books are paperbacks or hardcovers. In the details step you must tick the Low-content box under Categories; Amazon says books that skip this box, or choose a different one, will be rejected. In the content step you pick Publish without an ISBN or Use my own ISBN.

## ISBN and barcode

Without your own ISBN, KDP places a barcode in a 2 x 1.2 inch white box in the lower right corner of the back cover, so keep that area free of text and images. With your own ISBN you can supply your own barcode or let KDP add one.

## What you give up

- Expanded Distribution is not supported for low-content books, whichever ISBN option you choose.
- Read Sample and the back cover thumbnail are not shown for books without an ISBN.
- Low-content books cannot be part of a series.
- Release Date scheduling is not offered.

## Worked example

A lined journal published without an ISBN sells only through Amazon, gets an Amazon-placed barcode and has no Look Inside. The same journal with a purchased ISBN can show a reading sample, but it still cannot use Expanded Distribution or join a series.

## Common mistakes

- Repeating keywords such as "notebook" or "journal" in the title. Amazon's metadata guidelines prohibit repeating generic keywords in the title field.
- Printing art in the barcode box, which can get the file rejected.

Amazon changes these rules; the linked KDP help pages are the authority.
