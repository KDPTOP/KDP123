## The two definitions

Amazon's Content Guidelines draw the line by who created the content, not by how much AI was involved later.

- **AI-generated:** text, images or translations created by an AI-based tool. If AI produced the actual content, it is AI-generated even after substantial human edits.
- **AI-assisted:** content you created yourself and then edited, refined, error-checked or otherwise improved with AI tools. Using AI to brainstorm ideas also counts as AI-assisted, as long as you wrote the text or made the images yourself.

## What to disclose and when

KDP asks about AI-generated content when you publish a new book and when you edit and republish an existing one. The requirement covers three kinds of content: text, images (cover, interior images and artwork) and translations. AI-assisted content does not need to be disclosed.

## Worked examples

- A novel you wrote, run through an AI grammar checker, with a cover from a human designer: AI-assisted, nothing to disclose.
- The same novel with a cover made in an AI image generator: disclose AI-generated images.
- A nonfiction book whose chapters were drafted by a chatbot and then rewritten by you: disclose AI-generated text.
- Your own book translated into Spanish by an AI tool: disclose an AI-generated translation.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming heavy editing turns AI output into your own work. Under Amazon's definition, it does not.
- Forgetting the cover. Interior text may be yours while the artwork is AI-generated.
- Treating disclosure as approval. AI content still has to meet every other guideline, including copyright and trademark rules, and the publisher is responsible for checking it.

Amazon changes these rules; the linked KDP help pages are the authority.
