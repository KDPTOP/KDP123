## When differentiation is required

A public domain book is one no longer covered by copyright, usually because the rights expired. KDP allows these books, but if a free version of the title is already available in the store, it only accepts a differentiated version. Amazon recognizes three kinds:

- **Translated:** an original translation.
- **Annotated:** original annotations such as study guides, literary critiques, detailed biographies or historical context.
- **Illustrated:** 10 or more original illustrations relevant to the book.

## How to label the edition

The title field must include (Translated), (Annotated) or (Illustrated). "Pride and Prejudice (Annotated)" is acceptable; "Pride and Prejudice (with an Introduction by Tiffany Gordon)" is not. The description must open with a bullet-point summary of what is original, and Amazon's page attaches a maximum of 80 characters to that summary. In the Publishing Rights step, choose "This is a public domain work." Copyright terms vary by country, so a work may be public domain in one market but not another.

## Program limits

Ebooks that consist primarily of public domain content earn only the 35% royalty, cannot be enrolled in KDP Select, cannot be set up as pre-orders and cannot be used to create a series.

## Worked example

A $2.99 annotated classic with a 1 MB file sold to a US buyer earns 35% × $2.99 = $1.05. The same price on the 70% option would have paid about $1.99 after delivery costs, but that option is not available for this book.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a collection of several free classics as differentiated. Collections are on Amazon's "not differentiated" list.
- Writing "Illustrated" for a handful of images. The threshold is 10 or more original illustrations.

Amazon changes these rules; the linked KDP help pages are the authority.
